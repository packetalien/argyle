The Argyle Agency's Official Guide to Spotting a Digital Miscreant
(Abridged for the Modern Age, and the Easily Distracted Trainee)

Foreword: In these times of instantaneous communication via the electric telegraph and other such wonders, one must be ever vigilant for scoundrels who would misuse these marvels for their own dastardly ends. - S.H.

1. The Unsolicited Proposal: Does the message promise untold riches, true love from an unknown admirer, or a free puppy delivered by steam-powered drone? Approach with EXTREME skepticism. Free puppies are rarely free.

2. The Demand for Haste: Are you being URGED to act NOW? To click IMMEDIATELY? To send your bank details before tea time or face DIRE consequences? This is a classic rogue's tactic. Take a breath, brew a cuppa, and consult your nearest consulting detective.

3. The Suspicious Sender: Do you know the sender? Is their electronic mail address an unpronounceable jumble of letters and numbers, or does it claim to be from your Great Aunt Mildred but is hosted on a server in a country she's never heard of? Investigate further!

4. The Egregious Errors: Is the message riddled with spelling mistakes, grammatical faux pas, or anachronistic slang (e.g., "Your mainframe is totally pwned, dude!")? While some modern miscreants are surprisingly literate, many are not.

5. The 'Too Good To Be True' Attachment: Does it promise "Secrets of the Crown Jewels.docx" or "Free Money Exploit.exe"? Do NOT open it. It is more likely to contain a digital viper than valuable information.

Remember, trainees: Observation and deduction are your best defenses. And a strong password.
