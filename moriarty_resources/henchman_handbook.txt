A HENCHMAN'S HANDBOOK TO ARTFUL HENCHING
            (Third Edition - Now With Less Accidental Self-Sabotage!)

            Foreword by the Illustrious Professor J. Moriarty:
            It has come to my attention that the general quality of henching has declined significantly. This volume is my attempt to rectify this lamentable situation. Study it well, or face the consequences (which usually involve being a test subject for one of Colonel Von Strudel's less... reliable... contraptions).

            Chapter 1: The Art of Lurking Menacingly
            - Proper posture for maximum intimidation (shoulders back, scowl perfected).
            - Choosing the right dimly lit alleyway.
            - Synchronized glaring for team efforts.
            - Note: Avoid lurking near bakeries; the smell of fresh bread is not conducive to menace. (Unless you are attempting to steal pies. Pie-theft is a separate, and equally important, module. See Appendix B: 'Advanced Pie Piracy').

            Chapter 2: Basic Villainous Vocabulary
            - Essential phrases: "Yes, Master!", "Right away, Professor!", "They went thataway!" (use sparingly).
            - Forbidden phrases: "Are you sure this is a good idea?", "Oops.", "I think I left the doom-ray on."
            - The importance of maniacal laughter (practice in front of a mirror).

            Chapter 3: Captive Management (The Non-Lethal Edition - mostly)
            - Tying knots that look secure but can be easily escaped from by any competent hero (we need them for the sequel, you see).
            - Delivering exposition-laden taunts (see 'Advanced Monologue Writing').
            - Remembering to feed the captives (soggy bread and water is traditional, but consider their dietary restrictions – an unhappy captive is a noisy captive). (The Professor is considering upgrading to slightly stale biscuits, as a morale booster. Morale in henchmen is... surprisingly important for cannon fodder).

            Chapter 4: Understanding "The Plan"
            - "The Plan" is always capitalized. It is brilliant. It is infallible (until it isn't).
            - Your job is not to understand "The Plan," but to execute your small, often confusing, part of it.
            - If "The Plan" seems overly complicated and prone to failure, refer to Chapter 2, "Yes, Master!"

            Chapter 5: Retreating Strategically (Running Away)
            - There is no shame in a tactical withdrawal, especially if Sherlock Holmes is involved.
            - Pre-designated escape routes are your friend.
            - Avoid tripping over your own feet or other henchmen. It's undignified.

            Afterword: Go forth and hench with moderate competence! - J.M.
            (This document is for satirical and training purposes within a simulated environment only. Real-world henching is strongly discouraged and probably illegal.)
